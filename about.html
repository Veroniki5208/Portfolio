Hi, I’m Veronique.

I’m a Human Centered Design & Engineering student at the University of Washington and an emerging product designer. I use research, product thinking, and prototyping to turn confusing or high-friction experiences into ones people can navigate with confidence.

Graduating June 2027 · Seeking full-time Product Design, UX, and PM roles
THE FIRST INTERACTION I REMEMBER

When I was seven, I found a web game with one instruction: “Don’t Press It.”

Naturally, I pressed it. Again and again.

Every response surprised me. It was a small interaction, but it showed me that design can shape what people notice, feel, and choose to do. That curiosity has stuck with me ever since: how can a digital product invite exploration, reduce uncertainty, or help someone feel more confident?

HOW I WORK

I’m most energized by problems that initially feel unclear: a workflow people cannot navigate, a need that has been poorly defined, or a product decision that needs better evidence. I start by understanding the people and constraints involved, then translate what I learn into a focused problem, a testable direction, and a clearer experience.

My work spans user research, product strategy, interaction design, and prototyping. I care about making technology feel less intimidating—especially when people are rushed, distracted, uncertain, or using a product for the first time.

OUTSIDE OF WORK

Music — I play piano, guitar, and violin, and hope to compose original soundtracks.

Worldbuilding — I love sci-fi and fantasy, especially stories where small choices change entire systems.

Curiosity — I’m fascinated by how technology quietly shapes everyday behavior.

These interests shape how I design: I notice rhythm, consequence, emotion, and the small details that help an experience feel coherent.
